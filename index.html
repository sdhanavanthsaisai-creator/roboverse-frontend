02 — PROBLEM STATEMENT & SOLUTION ENGINE

Navigation under
dynamic constraints.

RB/25 must cross a maze it has never seen, carrying up to 5 kg, while routes seal and obstacles appear. Because the drivetrain is a three-wheel holonomic base, the rover can translate and rotate independently — the control problem reduces to three wheel speeds, solved exactly by inverse kinematics. Perception fuses four LiDAR pucks and one ultrasonic rangefinder into a log-odds occupancy grid; the planner runs A* on that grid and re-plans from the robot's current cell the moment the world invalidates its route. No human ever touches a joystick.

Shortest-looking is not fastest — cost, not distance, decides the route.
EQ 01 — HOLONOMIC FORWARD KINEMATICS

Three omni wheels at 90° / 210° / 330°

Each wheel contributes the body velocity projected on its roller axis plus the rotation term R·ω. Three independent equations, three unknowns — the base is fully holonomic.

EQ 02 — INVERSE KINEMATICS

Body twist → wheel speeds

J is square and never singular for this roller placement, so any commanded twist maps to exact wheel speeds — sideways sprints while facing the goal are legal moves.

EQ 03 — PLANNER · A*

Optimal grid search

Manhattan h is admissible on the 4-connected grid, so the returned route is provably minimal — the benchmark every re-plan is measured against.

EQ 04 — SLAM · OCCUPANCY UPDATE

Bayes in log-odds form

Every LiDAR return nudges a cell by a fixed log-ratio, so one noisy ping can never rewrite the map; certainty saturates instead of compounding.

EQ 05 — CONTROL · PID HEADING LOOP

Closing the loop at 100 Hz

Proportional kick, integral patience, derivative damping — u(t) becomes the ω command fed straight into the inverse kinematics block.
